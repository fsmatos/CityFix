# 0010: Firestore and Storage layout

Status: accepted (F-010, Gate 1 on 2026-11-10; Diogo agreed the schema before the Gate; rules tightened after the F-010 review on 2026-11-10, adding `tickets/{id}/reports/{uid}` for grouping; review round 2 added the server-timestamped `resolvedAt`). Rules drafted in `firestore.rules` and `storage.rules`, not deployed (Q5 in [decision 0011](0011-firebase-plan-notifications-close-maps.md)).

## Context
- Phase 1 kept everything in memory (`LocalTicketsRepository`, [decision 0009](0009-tickets-repository.md)). Every Firebase task (F-011..F-016, D-009..D-014) needs one shared data layout.
- Phase 1 left six schema risks open: attachment phase, FCM token and `role`, a public collection, NIF uniqueness, one municipality document, the reopen window.
- Firestore rules secure whole documents, not fields. Anonymous users must never see the author's personal data or exact coordinates (CLAUDE.md), so the anonymous view cannot be a filtered read of `tickets`.
- Brief IV wants reads and writes to work offline. Firestore transactions fail without a network; batched writes are queued.

## Decision

### Layout
Model types stay as [decision 0003](0003-domain-model-representation.md) defines them (pure Kotlin, `Long` times, enum names as strings). All mapping lives in `data/`. Firestore-only fields (`role`, `fcmTokens`, `participantIds`, `resolvedAt`, the public projection, `ticketLocations`) are not added to `model/`.

| Path | Fields | Who reads | Who writes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `users/{uid}` | `role` (`CITIZEN`/`OPERATOR`), `name`, `nif`, `email`, `phone`, `photoPath?`, `createdAt`; citizen only: `georeferencing` (GeoPoint), `registrationStatus` (`PENDING`/`ACTIVE`), `impactDetection`, `validatedBy?`, `validatedAt?`; both: `fcmTokens` (array) | the owner; operators | the owner at registration and for editable fields (never `name`, `nif`, `georeferencing`, brief I.2.C/I.3.C); operators set `registrationStatus`, `validatedBy`, `validatedAt` |

| `municipality/main` | `name`, `emailDomain` (lowercase, without `@`, for example `cm-coimbra.pt`), `center` (GeoPoint, Pr. Republica), `radiusKm` (10.0) | everyone, anonymous included | console only |
| `categories/{id}` | `name`, `active` | everyone | operators |

| `tickets/{id}/reports/{uid}` | `categoryId`, `situation`, `location` (GeoPoint, exact), `description`, `createdAt` | its author; operators | an active citizen joining the ticket by grouping, in the same batch as the `followerIds`/`participantIds` update; never updated |

| `ticketLocations/{id}` | `categoryId`, `situation`, `location` (GeoPoint, exact), `active` (bool, `true` while the ticket is not CLOSED) | signed-in active citizens; operators; never anonymous users | the creator in the same batch as `tickets/{id}` (`active: true`); `active: false` by an operator or participant only in the batch that closes the ticket; nothing once CLOSED |
| `stats/summary` | reserved for dashboard counters | decided in F-011 | decided in F-011 |
| Storage `tickets/{ticketId}/{attachmentId}.jpg\|mp4`, `tickets/{ticketId}/{attachmentId}_thumb.jpg` | - | everyone when the ticket's `scope` is PUBLIC (exactly when `publicTickets/{ticketId}` exists); otherwise participants and operators | participants and operators (the F-013 upload worker), not once CLOSED; `image/*` under 10 MB, `video/*` under 100 MB |
| Storage `users/{uid}/photo.jpg` | - | the owner; operators | the owner; `image/*` under 10 MB |

### Choices
- **`publicTickets` is a separate collection**, written only from `toAnonymousView()` in `data/`. Its rules allow only the listed keys, so author ids, descriptions and exact coordinates cannot be stored there.
- **`participantIds` (Q9)** = creator + followers, kept in sync with `followerIds` in the same write. One `whereArrayContains("participantIds", uid)` query lists "my tickets", and one rule (`request.auth.uid in participantIds`) guards the ticket, its messages and its media.
- **`ticketLocations` (Q8).** The 10 m grouping (Option A, [decision 0002](0002-grouped-reports-single-ticket.md)) needs exact locations of other people's tickets, but citizens cannot read other citizens' `tickets` and `publicTickets` has only coarse locations. `ticketLocations/{id}` holds the minimum for the check (category, situation, exact location, active), written in the same batch as the ticket. F-012 implements the check.
- **Grouping, never a free follow (brief I.2 obs.).** A citizen joins a ticket only by reporting a problem with the same category and situation within about 10 m of it; there is no "follow" button for any other ticket. Grouping does not depend on scope: PUBLIC and PRIVATE tickets both group. F-012 finds the match through `ticketLocations` and writes one batch: the citizen's report `tickets/{id}/reports/{uid}` (category, situation, exact location, description, time) and an `arrayUnion` of their own uid on `followerIds` and `participantIds`. The rules allow that update only when the ticket is not CLOSED, the uid is the caller's and is new, and the report written in the same batch (`getAfter`) has the category and situation of `ticketLocations/{id}` and lies in a bounding box of 0.0001 deg latitude and 0.00013 deg longitude (about 11 m each way at Coimbra, 40.2 N; rules have no trigonometry). The exact 10 m circle is checked by the client (F-012). The update needs no read of the ticket, so it works offline. The citizen's media are appended afterwards through the attachments rule, as a participant.
- **Lifecycle (brief I.3.E, II).** The operator controls the transitions (and adds resolution media). A participant may only (a) reopen a RESOLVED ticket within 5 days of the RESOLVED entry, with a non-empty `justification`, and (b) write the automatic RESOLVED -> CLOSED after 5 days ([decision 0011](0011-firebase-plan-notifications-close-maps.md), Q4). Every status change sets `currentStatus` to one of the five `TicketStatus` names, to a different value than before, and appends exactly one history entry written by the caller (`responsibleId` = self, `status` = the new `currentStatus`, `dateTime` not earlier than the previous entry and at most 5 minutes after the server time); earlier entries can never be rewritten. Nobody, operator included, may close before 5 days. A CLOSED ticket is locked for everyone (brief II.5, "nao admitindo mais alteracoes"): no status, attachment, message, report, Storage upload or `publicTickets`/`ticketLocations` change.
- **The 5-day window runs on server time at both ends.** When an operator sets RESOLVED, the same write sets `resolvedAt` with `FieldValue.serverTimestamp()`, and the rule requires `resolvedAt == request.time`, so the client cannot choose it; no other write may change it. The reopen check (`request.time <= resolvedAt + 5 days`) and the close check (`request.time > resolvedAt + 5 days`, for everyone) compare it with the server time of the later write, so a reopen queued offline that syncs after the window is denied. The history entry's own `dateTime` stays a client `Long` (bounded as above) and is not used for the window; clients compute the reopen window, the close-on-read (Q4) and the 24 h warning from `resolvedAt`. `resolvedAt` is a `Timestamp`, an exception to "times stay `Long`", because only a server timestamp gives the rules a time the client cannot fake.
- **Operators prove the municipality domain with a verified e-mail (brief I.3.A).** The operator role needs `email_verified == true` at registration and on every check (Firestore and Storage). D-009 sends the verification e-mail first and creates `users/{uid}` with `role: OPERATOR` only after the address is verified; MFA enrolment needs a verified e-mail anyway. `emailDomain` is lowercase without `@`, because the rule compares it with `email.lower().split('@')[1]`.
- **Anonymous users do not sign in (brief I.1, "nao autenticados").** The Anonymous Auth provider stays disabled, and the rules treat a token with `sign_in_provider == 'anonymous'` as not signed in, so it never gets more than the public reads.
- **`statusHistory` and `attachments` are arrays in the ticket.** `currentStatus` and the history must change in one write ([decision 0004](0004-denormalised-current-status.md)), and a ticket has few entries. Each attachment has a `phase` field (`REPORT`/`RESOLUTION`).
- **`messages` is a subcollection.** A thread grows without limit, is read only in the detail and never by anonymous users.
- **Offline writes use batched writes, never transactions.** Reporting (including joining a ticket by grouping) must work offline. Transactions are used only where the network is required anyway: the registration (NIF uniqueness) and the F-013 upload worker (network constraint).
- **Times stay `Long` epoch milliseconds**, not `Timestamp` (decision 0003): trivial mapping, rules compare numbers.
- **Coordinates are `GeoPoint`**, used in `data/` only.
- **Storage paths have no user ids.** Media of a public ticket are readable by anonymous users through cross-service rules (`firestore.exists` on `publicTickets/{ticketId}`); otherwise `firestore.get` on the ticket checks participants and operators.
- **`stats/summary` is reserved (Q11)**: counters written in the same batch as each ticket change are the likely answer for the anonymous dashboard, which cannot read private tickets. F-011 decides and adds its rules; until then the path is denied.
- **`Attachment.phase` in `model/` is not part of F-010 (Q10).** The Firestore field is fixed here; the `AttachmentPhase` enum is added by the first task that writes a RESOLUTION attachment (D-013), or by F-011 if Diogo agrees.

### Phase 1 risks
1. **Attachment phase:** the `phase` field on each attachment (model change per Q10).
2. **FCM token and role:** `fcmTokens` and `role` exist only in `users/{uid}`; `data/` maps `role` to `Citizen`/`Operator`.
3. **Public collection:** `publicTickets`, built only by `toAnonymousView()`.
4. **NIF uniqueness:** `nifs/{ROLE}_{nif}` created in the registration transaction; the `users` create rule checks it with `getAfter`, and the `nifs` create rule checks that the same transaction creates the caller's own new `users/{uid}` with that role and NIF. Registration is online.
5. **One municipality:** the single document `municipality/main`. The unmapped associations: `define` = every category belongs to it, `pertence a` = every operator belongs to it, `valida` = `validatedBy` on the user, `gere` = `StatusHistory.responsibleId`. An operator registration must use a **verified** email in `emailDomain` (rule); `emailDomain` is stored lowercase without `@`. D-009 verifies the e-mail before creating the operator's `users/{uid}`.
6. **Reopen window:** the Firestore-only ticket field `resolvedAt` (server timestamp of the change to RESOLVED); see the 5-day window choice above. The RESOLVED entry in `statusHistory` stays the record for traceability and the repair time (decision 0005).

### Rejected
- Subcollections for `statusHistory` and `attachments`: a batch for every transition and extra reads for the detail.
- One `tickets` collection read with field filtering for anonymous users: rules cannot hide fields.
- Two queries (`creatorId ==` and `followerIds array-contains`) merged on the client instead of `participantIds`.
- For the 10 m grouping: grouping only against the citizen's own tickets (defeats Option A); letting any citizen read full tickets (exposes descriptions and author ids); a Cloud Function (conflicts with no-server notifications, decision 0011).
- A `{latitude, longitude}` map instead of `GeoPoint`: equally simple, but no type in the console.
- `Timestamp` for times: extra mapping, against decision 0003.

## Consequences
- Every report writes two or three documents in one batch (`tickets`, `ticketLocations`, and `publicTickets` for PUBLIC tickets). Every status change that affects anonymous users updates `publicTickets` too.
- `followerIds` and `participantIds` must always change together, and only in the grouping batch with the citizen's report.
- The rules use `get`/`exists` per request (user role, ticket participants); Firestore allows 10 per single-document request and 20 per batch, enough for these rules.
- Storage rules may access **at most two Firestore documents per rules evaluation** (Firebase docs, "Enhance with Cloud Firestore" in https://firebase.google.com/docs/storage/security/rules-conditions, checked 2026-11-10). Every Storage rule reads at most `tickets/{id}` (once, passed to the checks) and the caller's `users/{uid}`. That is why the public-media check reads the ticket's `scope` instead of `firestore.exists` on `publicTickets`.
- **An offline RESOLVED gets the sync time.** `FieldValue.serverTimestamp()` written offline is resolved by the server when the write reaches it, so `resolvedAt` is the time the operator's device synced, not the time the operator tapped "resolve"; the 5-day window starts then. The local cache shows an estimate until the sync. The history entry keeps the device time, so the two can differ for an operator who resolves offline; accepted.
- A device clock more than 5 minutes fast cannot write a status change (history `dateTime` bound); the client should show the error rather than retry silently.
- Signed-in active citizens can see exact locations, category and situation of every ticket through `ticketLocations`, but no author, description or media. Accepted for Option A.
- Lifecycle writes: operators change `currentStatus`, `statusHistory` and `attachments`; participants change only `currentStatus` and `statusHistory`, for the justified reopen and the 5-day close. Every status batch also mirrors `currentStatus` (and `resolvedAt`) to `publicTickets` for PUBLIC tickets, and the rule checks the mirror equals the ticket's new status.
- The rules do not check which operator transitions follow which (for example OPEN -> IN_PROGRESS); D-008 keeps that order in the pure `model/` state machine. Only the five status names, "the status must change", "nothing after CLOSED", "CLOSED only 5 days after RESOLVED", "only operators resolve" and the participant limits are enforced.
- `createdAt` and the first history entry's `dateTime` on create are client times and are not checked by the rules; they feed only lists and the repair time, not the 5-day window.
- The attachments-only update (upload state, follower media) checks only that the list does not shrink; rules cannot loop over a list, so a participant could still edit an existing entry. Accepted; F-013 changes only `uploadState` of its own entries.
- In grouping, the rules trust the location the client puts in the report (they cannot know where the phone really is) and use a bounding box, not the exact 10 m circle. In plain words: any active citizen can read the category, situation and exact location of every ticket in `ticketLocations`, copy them into a report and so join any ticket, PRIVATE ones included, and then read its description, the creator's media and the messages. This follows the brief (I.2 obs.): grouping is automatic, whatever the scope, so the 10 m distance and the matching category and situation are the only gates. The client (F-012) uses the device's GPS fix, never a typed location.
- **F-013 must remove location metadata from videos before upload** (strip the MP4 location atom or re-encode), because media of public tickets are readable by anonymous users (brief I.1.C shows their images and videos). Photos keep the GPS EXIF stripping from F-009. Storage limits: `image/*` under 10 MB (photos and thumbnails), `video/*` under 100 MB; F-013 compresses to fit.
- The rules do not check that `coarseLocation` is really rounded; `toAnonymousView()` is the only writer by convention.
- The anonymous 10 km radius is not enforced by the rules (anonymous users have no location in the request); it stays a client check.
- The drafts are untested until the person runs them in the console Rules Playground or deploys them with OK (Q5).
